# Purpose

Raphael is a shared context layer for people and their agents. It organizes work and the thoughts, references, and experiences surrounding it so they can be retrieved together when needed.

This describes product intent, not currently shipped functionality.

## The problem

Work and its context live in different places. A feature may be tracked in GitHub, its constraints discussed in a Google Doc, and an important idea captured as a voice note. Finding the issue does not necessarily reveal the thinking that should inform its implementation.

Raphael gives that context a consistent home and a common discovery interface. Its purpose is actionable knowledge, not simply collecting more links.

## Who it serves

- People who want to capture ideas and experiences without maintaining an elaborate document workspace.
- Agents that need to locate relevant work and recover its surrounding context before helping with planning or execution.

The mobile app is the primary phone interface for quick capture and retrieval. A web client provides another way to access the same context, while agent-facing tools provide consistent access to areas, projects, and resources.

## The experience

Capture should feel closer to Google Keep than Notion: lightweight notes, flexible text formatting, and optional voice or image attachments. Areas and projects provide organization without becoming arbitrary editable pages themselves.

PARA informs that organization, while CODE informs the goal of moving captured information toward useful expression. These concepts guide the product without requiring a prescribed workflow for every kind of work.

A person should be able to record an idea for a feature today and ask an agent to retrieve the feature's relevant context tomorrow. The agent can find both locally stored thinking and explicit references to material elsewhere.

## What Raphael does not replace

GitHub, ClickUp, Google Docs, and other tools can remain the places where work or documents are managed. Raphael does not require all external content to be copied into its own storage or all updates to happen through its interface.

Integrations connect those tools to Raphael's organization. They can store content, summaries, or references according to their capabilities. People can also create areas, projects, and notes directly, without an integration.

Raphael is not a Notion-style system where every object is another page, nor is it a mandate to move all work into one tool. It brings context together while allowing different systems to retain their roles.
